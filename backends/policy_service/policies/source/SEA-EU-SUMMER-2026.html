Europe Summer Peak and Heat Protocol 2026

Seasonal rules for air-conditioning failures during official heat alerts and for overbooking relocations at European properties during the 2026 summer peak.

Seasonal policy — applies only to nights from 15 June 2026 to 15 September 2026.

1. Purpose and scope

SUM-1.1 This protocol sets temporary guest-compensation rules for all ReverieEscapes properties in the Europe region during the 2026 summer peak, when sustained high temperatures and full occupancy increase the impact of air-conditioning failures and the likelihood of overbooking. It applies to nights falling between 15 June 2026 and 15 September 2026 inclusive; nights outside that window are assessed under the base documents only.

SUM-1.2 This protocol supplements the Global Guest Refund and Compensation Policy (GLB-RF-2026) and the Europe Regional Refund Addendum (EU-RF-2026). In accordance with GLB-2.2 it prevails over base documents of the same or broader scope for the matters it addresses. Country addenda and hotel rules continue to apply to all other matters; where a country addendum expressly refers to this protocol (for example ES-3.2 in Spain), this protocol applies in its window.

SUM-1.3 Matters not addressed in this protocol, including eligibility, verification, refund method and timing, and the treatment of taxes and fees, remain governed by GLB-RF-2026, EU-RF-2026 and any applicable country or hotel rules.

2. Air conditioning and heat alerts

SUM-2.1 Where in-room air conditioning fails during an official heat alert in force for the property's location, the guest receives 75% of the nightly room rate for each affected night. A night is affected when the air conditioning was unavailable for more than 4 hours while the alert was in force on that day or night. Qualifying alerts are the orange or red level of the national weather service, including Météo-France vigilance canicule orange/rouge, AEMET aviso naranja/rojo, DWD Hitzewarnung (including extreme heat), the Ministero della Salute heatwave bulletin (bollettino ondate di calore) at level 2 or 3, UK Health Security Agency amber or red Heat-Health Alert, KNMI code oranje/rood and IPMA aviso laranja/vermelho.

SUM-2.2 Air-conditioning failures that do not coincide with an official heat alert are compensated under GLB-5.1 (50% of the nightly room rate where climate control is unavailable for more than 4 hours between 22:00 and 07:00), subject to any country or hotel rule.

SUM-2.3 The duty manager records the alert level, issuing service and reference (bulletin time or alert number) in the incident log when the failure is first reported. An alert that was in force but not recorded may be confirmed afterwards from the issuing service's published archive.

SUM-2.4 Where the guest is moved the same day to an equivalent or better air-conditioned room, GLB-5.6 applies and later nights are not compensated. Portable cooling units supplied by the hotel do not reduce the entitlement under SUM-2.1 unless they restore the room to a temperature of 26 °C or below within 2 hours.

SUM-2.5 GLB-5.8 continues to apply: where several incidents occur on the same night, only the highest single percentage is paid. Percentages apply to the nightly room rate excluding taxes and fees (GLB-5.9).

3. Overbooking and relocation

SUM-3.1 Where a property cannot honour a confirmed reservation because of overbooking (a "walk"), the guest is relocated to an equivalent or better hotel, the first night of the reservation is refunded at 100% of the nightly room rate, and transport to the relocation hotel (and back, where the guest returns for later nights) is paid by the property.

SUM-3.2 Relocation is arranged before the guest leaves the property. The relocation hotel must be of the same or higher category and, where possible, within 3 km or 15 minutes' travel. Reverie Circle Gold and Platinum members are relocated last and, where possible, to another ReverieEscapes property.

SUM-3.3 Any nights spent at the relocation hotel are not charged to the guest by ReverieEscapes beyond the original reservation rate; any difference is borne by the walking property. A walk is a hotel-caused incident and is verified by the front-office manager's entry in the incident log.

SUM-3.4 Local tourist or overnight taxes for a refunded walk night are refunded where a country or hotel rule provides (EU-3.2).

4. Approval authority

SUM-4.1 During the window, guest relations representatives may approve climate-related refunds (refunds under section 2 of this protocol, GLB-5.1 or a country or hotel climate rule) up to EUR 600 per stay; for the United Kingdom the limit is the GBP equivalent at the reference rates in Appendix A of GLB-RF-2026. Refunds above that amount follow the thresholds of EU-3.1.

SUM-4.2 All other refunds, including walk refunds under SUM-3.1, follow the approval thresholds of EU-3.1 and any more specific local threshold.

5. Process and communication

SUM-5.1 Requests are made and assessed under GLB-4 (including the 30-day request limit) and refunds are processed within 14 calendar days of approval (EU-2.1).

SUM-5.2 When a refund is granted under this protocol, the decision names the clause applied and, for SUM-2.1, the heat alert relied upon. The guest receives a copy of this protocol together with the other policy documents relied upon (GLB-9.2), in English or the local language on request (EU-5.1).

SUM-5.3 Regional Operations reviews all decisions under this protocol after the window closes. This protocol lapses automatically at the end of the night of 15 September 2026 and is not renewed except by a new seasonal document.
